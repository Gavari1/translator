Gavári Grammar

Learn the core structure of Gavári through clear rules, compact tables, and practical examples.

Overview

Gavári is designed to be direct and regular. Verbs do not change according to the subject. Tense and mood are shown with separate markers, plural nouns use a productive suffix, and meaning is usually built through straightforward word order and compounds.

No verb conjugation

The main verb keeps the same form with every subject.

Mi kailo · Ti kailo · Lin kailo

No required article

Gavári does not require a separate word for English a, an, or the.

fale = a house / the house

Basic Word Order

Core pattern

Subject · Verb · Object

The normal sentence order is subject first, then the verb, then the object or complement.

Mi kailo fanu.

Mi kailo fanu.

I eat food.

Yona haluo hona.

She likes water.

Min tavo kala.

We have money.

Io wero carlan.

He sees the cars.

Verb Markers

Marker Function Example Meaning
— present / general Mi kailo. I eat.
pa past Mi pa kailo. I ate.
fa future Mi fa kailo. I will eat.
wa conditional Mi wa kailo. I would eat.

Placement

The marker comes directly before the main verb: Mi pa gavo sap nuai.

Negation

Use noa before the verb

The negative word noa is placed before the verb or verbal expression.

Mi noa kailo.

Mi noa tavo kala.

I do not have money.

Mi noa polno gavo.

I cannot go.

Ti noa delo gavo.

You should not go.

Compounds

Gavári frequently creates new meanings by combining existing words. A hyphen keeps the parts easy to recognize.

fale + faru
↓
fale-faru
big house
hona + muia
↓
hona-muia
lake
pesta + fale
↓
pesta-fale
neighborhood
duku + panu
↓
duku-panu
volcano

Condition adjective suffix

The productive suffix -wa forms weather or condition adjectives: rualwa “rainy,” sneulwa “snowy,” and veruwa “sunny.”
